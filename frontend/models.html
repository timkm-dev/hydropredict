Models

How the wind turbine warning model was chosen and how well it performs. Models were compared on Farms A and B; Farm C was kept back for one final test. Every number comes from a frozen evaluation run in the repository.

How a warning is produced

InputTurbine SCADA data10-minute averages, 42 to about 220 sensors per turbine
→
1 · DetectAutoencoderRebuilds the readings it expects from a healthy turbine. The gap from reality is how unusual the turbine is.
→
2 · AlarmSustained deviation6-hour average above the healthy-data threshold; alarm after about 12 hours.
→
3 · ExplainWhere to lookSensors furthest from normal, grouped by component, with a suggested team.

Why the autoencoder replaced the Isolation Forest

On the same 37 Farm A and B datasets, the autoencoder raised alarms on 8 of 18 faults, against 1 for the Isolation Forest, and still raised none on the 19 normal periods.

Learns how sensors relateThe autoencoder rebuilds all sensors together, so a bearing temperature that is wrong for the current power and wind stands out. The Isolation Forest flags rare readings, and a healthy turbine in a storm is rare too.
Builds sustained evidenceThe earlier models flagged only about 3% of readings inside fault periods, so the alarm counter seldom reached 72. Smoothing the autoencoder's error over 6 hours gives steadier evidence.
Explains each alertEach sensor's share of the error shows where to look: the top 3 included the failed component in 7 of 18 faults, against 3.3 by chance. The Isolation Forest gives one score per reading.

Same farms and the same scoring code for all three. The autoencoder's two alarm settings were chosen on these farms, so its A+B score is slightly optimistic; on untouched Farm C it scored 0.578, still above both earlier models.
