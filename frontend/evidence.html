How to read this

An anomaly is a score above the frozen threshold. A fault is a recorded event. We count a warning only when an anomaly occurs within the six hours before a fault. A match in time does not establish a cause.

What this demonstrates

The pipeline can score readings, flag unusual behavior and map flags to recorded faults. The event comparison also exposes missed warnings and unavailable history.

What it does not establish

This is retrospective development evidence, not a live prediction or an operational accuracy claim. Fault windows can overlap. Normal references were publisher selected, and sensor meanings, timezone and equipment identities remain unverified.

Evidence source and comparison rules

A fault is adequately scored when at least 80% of expected five-minute slots in its preceding six hours have scores, with a score within ten minutes of the fault. Score gaps over 7.5 minutes break chart lines and alert episodes. A reading exactly at or after the fault is excluded from its warning count. The model and threshold have not been changed for this page.
